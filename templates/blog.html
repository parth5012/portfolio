How I built this portfolio with AI, and the part that lied to me

Parth Chawla · {{ now.year if now else '2026' }} · 4 min read

I started with a site that looked finished and proved nothing. It had three projects, a typewriter animation on my name, and a headline asking for an internship. I had spent weeks on the wrong thing: I was decorating a page instead of making a claim.

So I did the unglamorous part first. I wrote one sentence I could actually defend — that I build AI systems which keep working on messy data because I measure them — and then threw away the three projects that could not support it. What replaced them is five cases pulled out of my own repositories, each one honest about what broke.

What AI did that I could not have done alone was the code. Every template, the styling, the contact endpoint with its validation and rate limiting, the twenty-six tests behind it. I wrote none of that by hand, and I could not have shipped it in the time I had. What that bought me was time to spend on the parts that are actually mine: writing the eval set, deciding what to leave out, and choosing to publish the embarrassing findings instead of quietly dropping them.

And here is the part I keep thinking about. My contact form lied to me. It said "Message sent!" and dropped every message on the floor. There was no server call at all, just a timer. A stranger would have trusted it. I only found it because I tried to break my own site on purpose, which is now a page on this site (/reliability) instead of a shrug.

The lesson was not "AI is unreliable." It was that a plausible-looking demo path is the exact thing an AI will happily build for you, and the only defence is trying to break your own work before someone else does.
